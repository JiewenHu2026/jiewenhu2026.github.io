Leaving Traces

Transforming Past Wounds into Future Strength

Leaving Traces proposes collective ecological restoration as a form of remembrance. At ChesLen Preserve, historical storytelling and the shared care of living landscapes connect the Underground Railroad’s legacy of mutual aid with future generations.

Project statement

In Chester County, the history of the Underground Railroad runs through a landscape of fields, wooded slopes, and creek valleys. For people escaping slavery, these environments could offer concealment, expose movement, or influence where and when they could travel. Their journeys depended on a close reading of the land and networks of mutual aid. Today, this history meets a changing landscape, where erosion, flooding, and habitat degradation call for renewed collective care.

Leaving Traces proposes ecological restoration as a living practice of remembrance. With ChesLen Preserve as a pilot for the Brandywine Greenway, the project brings residents, students, historians, and land stewards together to care for the places through which these histories are understood. Together, they would restore serpentine grasslands, create stormwater basins that support birds, and build woody structures that slow runoff, capture sediment, and improve firefly habitat. Shared labor becomes a way to carry the legacy of mutual aid into the present landscape.

Memory is encountered through the body: in the movement between open fields and wooded slopes, the sound of an owl beyond the trees, or the changing visibility of a woodland at dusk. Guided walks bring these encounters into conversation with documented accounts of movement, concealment, and survival. Seasonal change gives each return a different character, allowing historical interpretation and ecological observation to unfold together.

Over time, the work leaves traces of its own. A tended grassland, a planted slope, or a pool that holds water records acts of care that others will inherit. Seasonal stewardship and commemorative gatherings invite each generation to return, learn, and contribute. Remembrance takes shape through this repeated exchange between people and place, carrying the Underground Railroad’s legacy of mutual aid into the care of future landscapes.

Storytelling Center

The Storytelling Center proposes a temporary place for histories to be encountered and carried onward. Walls assembled from locally salvaged timber enclose a floor inscribed with gathered historical accounts and oral memories of the Underground Railroad. Above them, a suspended wax ceiling places the room within the changing light and warmth of each day.

In the proposed material cycle, sunlight gradually warms and softens the wax. As it descends, the ceiling opens to more light while the words below become obscured. What makes the room more visible also makes its stories less legible. Returning visitors encounter an altered surface: a passage read one day may be partly covered the next, until accumulated wax buries the inscriptions.

The enclosure, too, is conceived to weather and eventually disappear within the continuing cycles of day and night. Its impermanence holds the vulnerability of memory in view. As the physical traces recede, the work of remembrance passes to those who have read, listened, and gathered here. What remains depends on the stories they carry beyond the walls, and on their willingness to tell them again.
